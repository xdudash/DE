# Product Vision

## Definition
DeutschGo is a structured, comprehensive German-learning system that helps people build usable German for everyday life, work, and study, while allowing them to choose topics freely without losing the logic of a coherent learning path.

## Audience
Primary audiences are adult and young-adult learners who need German for migration, employment, education, integration, or daily life across Germany, Austria, and Switzerland (DACH). The product should serve absolute beginners and progress toward advanced competence rather than treating A1 as the end state.

## Product promise
A learner should be able to understand why a German form works, recognize it in authentic context, practise it, use it independently, and revisit it when needed. Progress should reflect demonstrated understanding, not merely time spent or streaks.

## Product pillars

### Connected knowledge of German
Represent grammar, morphology, syntax, vocabulary, collocations, pronunciation, pragmatics, register, discourse, spelling, and regional variation. Link concepts by prerequisites, contrast, use, and examples. CEFR levels organize learning but do not replace the underlying model of the language.

### Deep, pedagogically sound learning
Explanations should be accurate, contextual, level-aware, and clear about variation. Relevant examples, exceptions, common learner errors, and opportunities to apply the knowledge accompany a rule. Exercises test their stated objectives and explain answers.

### Freedom with guidance
Learners can search, browse, jump to topics, choose interests, and change goals. The system shows prerequisites and likely gaps; it recommends a next step without trapping the learner in a rigid linear course.

### Four skills plus language knowledge
Support reading, listening, writing, and speaking alongside explicit grammar and vocabulary work. Connect skills to shared concepts and goals. Do not claim text-only exercises measure speaking or listening ability.

### Multilingual explanations
Separate German learning content from the language used to explain it. The initial product is intended to support 5–10 explanation/interface languages. The exact set, translation workflow, and release coverage must be chosen explicitly. Machine-generated translations require review before being treated as authoritative.

### Useful, responsibly sourced materials
Use original content and external resources where reuse is lawful and technically appropriate. Record license/terms, attribution, provenance, version, and permitted use. A public URL does not automatically permit copying or redistribution.

### DACH-aware German
Explain standard German and flag meaningful differences in vocabulary, pronunciation, conventions, and usage among Germany, Austria, and Switzerland. Label regional language clearly and avoid overstating differences.

## Principles
- Understanding over gamification.
- Reusable concepts over duplicated content.
- User agency plus intelligent guidance.
- Transparent evidence of progress.
- Accessible, responsive design.
- Modular, maintainable development by one founder using AI tools.
- Scale responsibly for the first 1,000+ users without premature distributed-system complexity.
- Privacy and data minimization from the beginning.

## Long-term ambition
Progress from a validated core learning loop to a comprehensive knowledge graph, a high-quality curriculum across CEFR levels, curated reading/listening resources, adaptive practice, learner analytics, and a sustainable free-plus-subscription business. Completeness is a multi-stage objective, not a one-prompt outcome.

## Non-goals
DeutschGo is not merely a flashcard app, a fixed sequence of shallow lessons, an unrestricted AI wrapper, a scraper for copyrighted courseware, or a streak system that confuses activity with competence.
